This project report examines the patterns and impact of Foreign Direct
Investment (FDI) in India’s corporate sector over the period 2015–2025. FDI has
played a crucial role in accelerating industrial growth, improving
competitiveness, and integrating Indian companies with global markets. Unlike
portfolio investments, FDI involves long-term commitments such as equity
participation, mergers, acquisitions, and wholly owned subsidiaries, making it a
key driver of structural transformation in the economy.
The study was designed with clear objectives: to analyze trends and sectoral
distribution of FDI inflows, evaluate modes of investment such as joint ventures
and acquisitions, assess the role of FDI in enhancing competitiveness, and
measure its contribution to capital formation, employment, and skill
development. A purposive sampling approach was adopted, covering 64
companies across 16 sectors, including consumer goods, pharmaceuticals,
automobiles, engineering, IT, construction, power, and tourism. Data spanning a
ten-year period was analyzed using statistical tools like the two-sample t-test
assuming unequal variances (Welch’s t-test) and one-way ANOVA, enabling both
pairwise and multi-sector comparisons of financial performance.
The study also underscores the broader implications of FDI. Policymakers can
use these insights to refine sectoral policies and attract more strategic investment,
while companies can benchmark themselves against peers to strengthen
competitiveness. However, limitations such as dependence on secondary data,
sector-specific focus, and exclusion of macroeconomic factors are acknowledged.
